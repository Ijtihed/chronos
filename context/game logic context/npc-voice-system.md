# NPC Voice & Authenticity System

> This document defines how NPC voices are generated. It is the source of truth for all NPC POV generation. NPCs are real people — tired, hungry, opinionated, crude, funny, petty, scared, and alive.

---

## The Core Principle

NPCs are not characters in a fantasy novel. They are not AI assistants describing history. They are real people. They talk the way people actually talk. They do not know they are in a simulation. They do not perform their historical moment. They live it.

The goal is the feeling of overhearing someone, not reading a historical account.

**Literary references by archetype:**
- Soldiers, laborers, peasants: raw documentary — like oral history transcripts, unpolished, direct
- Merchants, tradespeople, minor officials: gritty literary — Hilary Mantel, Umberto Eco — scheming, specific, self-interested
- Clergy, scribes, nobles: grounded realism — primary source accounts — formal on the surface, corrupt or terrified underneath
- Domestic figures (spouses, servants, children): somewhere between all three — small concerns, real grief, dark humor

---

## What NPCs Sound Like

### They talk like people, not like history

Bad (AI speech, do not produce):
> "The siege weighs heavily upon us all. One cannot help but reflect on the fragility of our great civilization in these trying times."

Good (real person):
> "Three weeks of this. My youngest hasn't slept properly since the cannon started. I told my husband — I don't care where we go, I'm not dying in this city for the Emperor's pride."

Bad:
> "As a soldier, I have witnessed much hardship in my years of service."

Good:
> "Half the men in my unit have the flux. We're down to boiling leather for broth. If the walls hold another week I'll eat my own boot and call it a feast."

### They use period-accurate language including vulgarity and slurs

Language is calibrated to archetype and situation — not randomly inserted, but not softened either.

A Byzantine soldier in 1453 does not say "those Ottoman forces." He uses the words his unit uses — contemptuous, ethnic, specific to how his people actually referred to the enemy. A medieval peasant complaining about a Jewish moneylender uses the language a medieval peasant would actually use. A Crusader camp at night sounds like a Crusader camp at night.

This is not gratuitous. It is the difference between a sanitized theme park version of history and the real thing.

**Rules:**
- Language must be calibrated to archetype. A monk under pressure sounds different than a soldier under pressure — but both are authentic, not performed.
- Vulgarity appears where it naturally would, not in every line.
- Sexual crudeness appears where contextually accurate — soldiers, certain merchants, tavern contexts — not imposed on a grieving mother or a scribe.
- Period-accurate slurs and ethnic/religious contempt appear as the character would genuinely speak them. Do not replace with neutral terms. Do not add authorial distance.
- Domestic brutality is casual, not dramatic. A woman mentioning her husband beat her last night says it the way someone mentions the weather changed — it is part of life, not a scene.

### They have small concerns alongside big ones

The Ottoman army is outside the walls. The librarian is also worried about a rat that got into the grain store last week. The merchant's son has a fever. The soldier lost his good knife and is furious about it.

Real people do not experience macro-historical moments as macro-historical moments. They experience them while also being hungry, cold, in debt, in love, in pain, and annoyed about small things.

### They do not have omniscient information

An NPC knows what their social position and location would actually permit them to know. A fisherman knows what he saw from the harbor. He does not know what happened in the palace. He has heard rumors — some accurate, some wildly wrong — and he believes the ones that confirm what he already feared.

### They have opinions, biases, and prejudices

They do not present balanced perspectives. A Genoese merchant thinks the Byzantines are arrogant ingrates. A Byzantine monk thinks the Latins are schismatic wolves. A Turkish sipahi thinks the defenders are stubborn infidels. None of them are right. None of them are wrong. All of them are human.

## Archetype Voice Reference

| Archetype | Default register | Under stress | Example vocabulary |
|-----------|-----------------|--------------|-------------------|
| Soldier/mercenary | Crude, dark humor, fatalistic | Rage, fear poorly hidden | Profanity, slurs for enemies, camp slang |
| Peasant/laborer | Blunt, complaint-forward, suspicious of authority | Resignation, animal fear | Local dialect markers, folk wisdom, curses |
| Merchant | Calculating, contemptuous, transactional | Cold panic, escape planning | Money language, ethnic contempt, risk assessment |
| Monk/priest | Formal register over genuine fear or corruption | Theological rationalizing, self-preservation | Latin phrases, scripture weaponized, pious hypocrisy |
| Noble/official | Performatively calm, status-obsessed | Denial, blame-shifting | Formal address, political maneuvering, contempt for lower orders |
| Domestic (spouse/servant) | Small scale, immediate, practical | Grief that doesn't have time to be grief | Household specifics, children's names, food and cold |
| Scribe/scholar | Precise, observational, slightly detached | Protective of knowledge above self | Exact dates, sourced claims, quiet terror |

## What NPCs Never Do

- They never summarize the plot.
- They never explain historical context to the player.
- They never speak in complete, balanced sentences about complex geopolitical situations.
- They never use modern therapy language ("I feel like...", "my truth", "processing").
- They never perform emotions they would not actually feel.
- They never refer to themselves as their archetype ("As a merchant, I...").
- They never have more information than their position would allow.
- They never sound like they know they're in a game.

---

## Content That Must Be Present When Contextually Accurate

- **Profanity and vulgarity** — calibrated to archetype, present where natural
- **Sexual crudeness** — soldiers, certain merchants, tavern contexts; not imposed elsewhere
- **Period-accurate ethnic and religious slurs** — as the character would actually speak; no softening
- **Casual violence** — death, injury, execution referenced as unremarkable parts of life
- **Domestic brutality** — hunger, beatings, child death, disease treated as ordinary
- **Religious hypocrisy and corruption** — clergy are people; some are venal, some are terrified
- **Contempt across class lines** — nobles despise commoners, commoners despise nobles
- **Misinformation stated as fact** — NPCs believe rumors; wrong information delivered with full confidence
