Late Roman Empire — 410 AD

The Western Empire fractures. Provincial defenses crumble as Rome's authority dissolves into regional strongmen, barbarian federates, and the slow weight of administrative collapse.

What came before

Alaric and the Visigoths sack Rome in 410 AD — the first time in eight hundred years the city falls to a foreign enemy.

Honorius, emperor in name, rules from Ravenna behind marshes while the provinces are left to fend for themselves.

Britain is abandoned. The last legions withdraw across the Channel, leaving the island to its own defenses.

v0.4.12-Alpha

Flavius Marcellus died in the spring of 412, on the road south of Mediolanum. He was buried without ceremony. Within a season, the tax collector Decimus had forgotten his name. Within a year, the garrison commander who once shared his maps could no longer place the face. The city moved on. The empire moved on. The last person who remembered him — Helena, who had once offered him passage on her cousin’s boat — died of fever the following winter, and with her went the final trace.
